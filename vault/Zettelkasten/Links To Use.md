Price's law is a bibliometric hypothesis that states, in any domain the square root of people in it do 50% of the work. This is a generally more well known "law" but it's accuracy is lacking. In truth it is known that the actual distribution is more skewed, showing that even less people than predicted by Price's law are doing most of the work. A good example would be wealth. Almost all of the world's wealth is concentrated in the hands of the few (and as we know the number is significantly lower than sqrt(8B)), a fact which we all know quite well. Price's law is just a generalization on this observation.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVtCO84MDj8 - Khan Academy and the Effectiveness of Science Videos

In my youthful teenage years, I loved seeing science videos and "learning" from them. You know, these youtube videos that pops up every once in a while trying their best to explain some new concept or idea. You watch them, believing you have absorbed the idea; you try to remember what was it that you learned a couple days later and... NOTHING. In this video Derek Muller argues that the problem with science videos that focus on the solution or idea, without challenging the viewer first causes most students to NOT UPDATE their view of the world, so it passes through their ears, learning nothing. Only true exploration of the subject and challenging the beliefs the students have can cause them to update their view of the world, thus, learning to commence. From my experience above I believe this is likely true.

Think about the creator of these recipes. To reach a recipe need to experiment with ingredients and cooking techniques, looking for what to improve.
