Musings on our future with AI

> I want AI to do my laundry and dishes so that I can do art and writing, not for the AI to do my art and writing so that I can do my laundry and dishes. [see: [Joanna Maciejewska](https://x.com/AuthorJMac/status/1773679197631701238)]

The year is 2026, as I am writing these words many people are fearful of the coming age. Some of my friends are worried they are wasting their time on higher-education, and I 22, I need to choose the path of my future in the time where everything seems to change so rapidly. This post will mainly focus on one self-centered question -- What then, should I do in the future?

My hope is that you, if you are asking yourself a similar question, will find the time to not only ask it, but to answer it fully.

But why should I care? The answer will depend on you. If you are someone who doesn't have a goal, yet strives for excellence, Hamming might provide you with a compelling reason: 

> To illustrate the importance of this point of trying to foresee the future I often use a standard story. It is well known the drunken sailor who staggers to the left or right with n independent random steps will, on the average, end up about sqrt(n) steps from the origin. But if there is a pretty girl in one direction, then his steps will tend to go in that direction and he will go a distance proportional to n. In a lifetime of many, many independent choices, small and large, a career with a vision will get you a distance proportional to n, while no vision will get you only the distance sqrt(n). In a sense, the main difference between those who go far and those who do not is some people have a vision and the others do not and therefore can only react to the current events as they happen. [see: The Art Of Doing Science And Engineering, Richard Hamming pp 6]

You then can argue the future with it's endless possibilities is unpredictable, and this excersise futile; but that would be missing the point. Any direction as argued by Hamming is better than no direction at all, or as he puts it: 

> No vision, not much of a future. [see: The Art Of Doing Science And Engineering, Richard Hamming pp 7]

Now, if you are someone who does not believe in Hamming's way of life -- where you should have goals and strive for excellence, his reasoning might fail you. This post was not at all made with this motivation in mind. I believe the majority of people reading this will not concern themselves with any particular vision for their future beyond having a stable job and family, and I am not here to preach one. What is it then that concerns me and many others? 

For me at least FEAR.

when hearing of AI taking jobs and getting better at taking more and more jobs, you ask yourself "will it stop?" and the conclusion I have reached is "unlikely". Because of that I became afraid.
